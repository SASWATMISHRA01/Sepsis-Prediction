      How to Lower Sepsis Risk & Path to Recovery

      Evidence-based clinical steps that reduce sepsis risk score and restore patient health

      💉

      1. Early Targeted Antibiotic Therapy

      Blood cultures are collected before antibiotics are started. Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics are administered within 1 hour of sepsis suspicion. Once lab sensitivity results are available (24–48 h), therapy is de-escalated to a narrow-spectrum agent, reducing organ stress and lactate levels.

      ↓ WBC, ↓ CRP, ↓ Procalcitonin within 48–72 hrs
      🩸

      2. Aggressive IV Fluid Resuscitation

      Crystalloid fluids (e.g., Normal Saline or Lactated Ringer's) are given at 30 mL/kg over 3 hours to restore circulating blood volume, raise MAP above 65 mmHg, and improve organ perfusion. Fluid balance is reassessed every hour.

      ↑ MAP, ↓ Lactate, ↑ Urine output → organ recovery
      🫀

      3. Vasopressor Support (if needed)

      If MAP remains below 65 mmHg after fluids, Norepinephrine is started as a first-line vasopressor via a central line. It raises blood pressure to maintain perfusion to vital organs (heart, brain, kidneys) while the infection is being treated.

      ↑ Systolic BP, ↓ Shock, Stabilised Heart Rate
      🫁

      4. Oxygen Therapy & Respiratory Support

      Supplemental oxygen is provided to maintain SpO2 above 94%. Patients with severe respiratory failure may require high-flow nasal oxygen, NIV, or mechanical ventilation. Respiratory rate normalises as infection resolves and oxygenation improves.

      ↑ SpO2, ↓ Respiratory Rate → improved breathing
      🧪

      5. Source Control & Lab Monitoring

      The infection source (wound, abscess, catheter, IV line) is identified and removed or surgically drained. Serial labs (lactate, WBC, creatinine, bilirubin) are repeated every 6–12 hours to track organ recovery. Trending improvement confirms treatment is working.

      ↓ Lactate, ↓ Creatinine → reduced organ dysfunction
      🌡️

      6. Fever & Glucose Management

      Antipyretics (Paracetamol) are used to bring temperature below 38.3°C, reducing metabolic demand and heart rate. Blood glucose is maintained between 140–180 mg/dL using insulin protocols — hyperglycemia worsens outcomes in septic patients.

      ↓ Temperature, ↓ HR, Stable Glucose → metabolic recovery
      🛏️

      7. ICU Monitoring & Organ Support

      Continuous hemodynamic monitoring (arterial line, central venous pressure) allows real-time tracking. Renal replacement therapy (dialysis) may be initiated if creatinine rises sharply. Platelet transfusions are given if platelet count falls below 50 K/μL.

      ↑ Platelets, ↓ Creatinine, Stable vitals → organ recovery
      🌿

      8. Nutrition, Rehabilitation & Recovery

      Early enteral nutrition (within 24–48 h) prevents gut barrier breakdown. Once infection is controlled, antibiotics are stopped (typically after 7–10 days), vasopressors are weaned, and the patient begins physiotherapy. Immune function improves and risk score drops steadily toward the Low / Safe range.

      ↓ Overall Risk Score → Patient discharged healthy
      ℹ️

      Note: This pathway is based on the Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines. All clinical decisions must be made by a qualified physician based on individual patient assessment. Early intervention is the single most important factor in reducing sepsis mortality.
